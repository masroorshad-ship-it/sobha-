Dubai is one of the most popular places for Indian families to buy a second home. The process is simpler than most people expect — here is how it works, step by step.

Can Indians buy property in Dubai?

Yes. Foreign nationals, including Indian residents and NRIs, can buy property with full ownership (freehold) in Dubai’s designated areas. You do not need a local partner or a UAE visa to buy. All the Sobha Realty projects on this website are open to international buyers.

Step by step: buying an off-plan home

  1. Choose the project and unit. Compare locations, layouts and payment plans. Download the brochures and floor plans from each project page.
  2. Reserve the unit. Pay the booking amount stated in Sobha Realty’s sales offer to hold your chosen home.
  3. Sign the Sale and Purchase Agreement (SPA). This is the contract with the developer. It lists the price, payment plan and expected completion date.
  4. Register with the Dubai Land Department (DLD). Off-plan homes are registered in the DLD’s Oqood system. The DLD registration fee is 4% of the price plus small admin fees, unless a developer offer covers it.
  5. Pay instalments according to the payment plan, while the building is constructed.
  6. Handover and title deed. On completion you pay the final instalment, receive the keys and get your title deed.

How to send money from India

Resident Indians usually pay through the RBI’s Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS), which currently allows up to USD 250,000 per person per financial year. Your bank handles the transfer with Form A2 and your PAN.

  • Many families buy jointly, so that each co-owner can use their own limit. Your bank will confirm what is allowed.
  • Tax Collected at Source (TCS) may apply on large remittances. It can generally be adjusted against your income tax, but it affects your cash flow.
  • NRIs living abroad can usually pay from their overseas income.

This is why payment plans matter: they spread the payments over the construction period. For example, Sobha Central offers 40% during construction and 60% on completion, while Sobha Elwood and The Woods at Sobha Sanctuary offer 60% during construction and 40% on completion.

Rules and rates can change. Please confirm the current LRS limit, TCS rate and documents with your bank or chartered accountant before you transfer money.

Costs to plan for

  • DLD registration fee: 4% of the property price, plus admin fees.
  • Registration and developer admin fees for the SPA and Oqood.
  • Service charges: yearly community fees after handover.
  • Currency conversion and bank charges on each transfer.

Documents you need

  • Passport copy for every buyer.
  • PAN and address proof for remittances from India (your bank may ask for more).
  • Email and phone number for the agreement and payment notices.

Taxes in the UAE and India

The UAE currently has no annual property tax and no personal income tax on rent. In India, resident taxpayers must report foreign assets in their income tax return (Schedule FA) and may owe Indian tax on rental income or gains. Speak to your chartered accountant about your own situation.

Frequently asked questions

Can I buy property in Dubai without travelling there?

Yes. Most of the process — choosing a home, signing the agreement and paying instalments — can be done remotely. Our team can meet you on a call or video meeting and arrange a site visit whenever you are in the UAE.

Can NRIs buy property in Dubai?

Yes. NRIs can buy freehold property in Dubai and usually pay from their overseas income or bank accounts. Resident Indians pay through the RBI’s Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS).

What is the minimum budget for a Sobha Realty home?

On this website, the lowest starting price is at The Woods, Sobha Sanctuary: one bedroom homes from AED 999K (about ₹2.2 crore*). Sobha Central starts from AED 1.78 Million (about ₹4 crore*).

Can I get a home loan for Dubai property?

UAE banks do lend to non-residents, mostly for ready or nearly finished homes and with a down payment. For off-plan homes, most buyers use the developer’s payment plan instead. Ask your bank and our advisor for options.
